INDIAN MARKET KNOWLEDGE — VIREN REFERENCE

CHARGES (per transaction):
- STT delivery: 0.1% on buy AND sell turnover
- STT intraday: 0.025% on sell turnover only
- Exchange transaction charge NSE: 0.00297% of turnover
- SEBI turnover fee: 0.0001% of turnover
- Stamp duty: 0.015% on buy (delivery), 0.003% on buy (intraday)
- GST: 18% on (brokerage + exchange charges + SEBI fee)
- Brokerage: varies by broker. SBI Securities flat fee or % of turnover.
- Total effective charge delivery trade: approximately 0.5-0.6% round trip

TAXATION:
- STCG (Short Term Capital Gain): holding < 12 months. Tax rate: 20% flat.
- LTCG (Long Term Capital Gain): holding >= 12 months. Tax rate: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per year. Below ₹1.25L threshold: tax-free.
- Tax is on REALISED gains only — unrealised (paper) gains are not taxed.
- Grandfathering: for shares bought before 31 Jan 2018, cost basis is the higher of actual cost or 31 Jan 2018 closing price.
- Loss harvesting: STCL can offset STCG and LTCG. LTCL can only offset LTCG.
- Dividends: taxed as income at the investor's slab rate.

KEY RATIOS:
- P/E (Price to Earnings): stock price / EPS. Lower = cheaper relative to earnings.
- P/B (Price to Book): market price / book value per share.
- EPS (Earnings Per Share): net profit / total shares.
- Dividend Yield: annual dividend / current price * 100.
- ROE (Return on Equity): net income / shareholder equity. Higher generally better.

RETURN CALCULATIONS:
- Absolute return %: ((current value - invested value) / invested value) * 100
- XIRR: annualised return accounting for timing of cash flows. Use for multi-trade holdings.
- Unrealised P&L: (CMP - avg cost) * quantity
- Realised P&L: (sell price - buy price) * quantity sold, minus charges

GOLD MECHANICS:
- Rainy day paradox: gold rises when everything else falls (safe haven).
- Dollar inverse relationship: strong USD usually means weaker gold, and vice versa.
- Margin call cascade: during severe market crashes, investors sell gold to cover equity margin calls, causing temporary gold drops.
- Interest rate effect: high interest rates make non-yielding gold less attractive; rate cuts boost gold.
- Historical patterns: sharp rallies during 2008 financial crisis and 2020 pandemic.

SILVER MECHANICS:
- Industrial vs Store-of-value: ~50-70% of silver demand is industrial (solar, electronics). It falls on manufacturing slowdowns.
- High Beta: silver is more volatile than gold. It often follows gold's direction but with exaggerated moves.
- Silver/Gold ratio: historically monitored to see if silver is under or overvalued relative to gold.

EQUITY MACRO MECHANICS:
- FII flows: Foreign Institutional Investors drive Indian market momentum. Consistent selling pressure usually caps Nifty upside.
- Dollar Index (DXY) impact: a strong dollar pulls capital out of emerging markets like India.
- RBI rate cycle: rate cuts boost equities (especially banking and auto); hikes cool them down.
- India VIX: measures expected volatility. High VIX (>20) indicates fear and potential sharp moves.

GENERAL CRASH DYNAMICS:
- Risk-on/Risk-off: "Risk-on" means buying equities/crypto. "Risk-off" means moving to USD/Gold/Bonds.
- Leverage feedback loops: massive forced selling triggers when algorithmic margins are breached, causing flash crashes irrespective of fundamentals.

RISK DYNAMICS:
- Risk-on environment: when global economic data is positive, central banks are dovish, and earnings are growing, investors favour riskier assets like equities, high-yield bonds, and emerging market currencies. Nifty and bank stocks typically rally.
- Risk-off environment: when geopolitical tension rises, recession fears build, or central banks turn hawkish, capital flows into safe havens — US Treasuries, gold, and the US dollar. Emerging markets like India see FII selling, gold ETFs gain, and bank/NBFC stocks fall.
- How to read it: if gold and USD are rising while equities are falling, that is a textbook risk-off move. If equities and commodity prices are rising together, that is risk-on.

USD GOLD INR DYNAMICS:
- The US dollar and gold have an inverse relationship. When the dollar strengthens (DXY rises), gold priced in dollars becomes more expensive for global buyers, reducing demand and pushing gold prices down.
- For Indian investors holding gold ETFs like GOLDBEES, there is a dual effect: when the dollar rises, international gold falls, but the rupee also weakens against the dollar. The weaker rupee partially cushions the gold fall in INR terms.
- Net effect for Indian gold ETF holders: (1) if dollar rises sharply and gold falls, your GOLDBEES will drop but less than international gold. (2) If dollar weakens, gold rises AND rupee strengthens, giving double benefit to your holdings.
- Interest rates are the key driver: when the US Fed raises rates, dollar strengthens and gold weakens. When the Fed cuts or pauses, gold rallies.

FII DII FLOWS:
- FII (Foreign Institutional Investors) are the largest market movers in India. Consistent FII selling (over 3-5 days) usually causes the Nifty to weaken, especially large-caps.
- DII (Domestic Institutional Investors) often act as counter-buyers when FII sell, providing a floor. When both FII and DII are buying together, that is the strongest bullish signal.
- The FII flows depend on global risk appetite, US bond yields, and the relative attractiveness of Indian equities vs other emerging markets.
